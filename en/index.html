The technique

What is scalp micropigmentation?

Scalp micropigmentation — also known as SMP or scalp micropigmentation— is a non-surgical aesthetic technique that uses tiny pigment dots on the scalp to visually recreate the appearance of hair follicles and change how areas of hair loss are perceived.

It does not generate new hair or physically increase the number of follicles. Its effect is visual: it can reduce the contrast between hair and scalp when hair is still present, or recreate the appearance of freshly shaved hair when the loss is more extensive.

The key is not simply to create dots. For the result to blend naturally, it is necessary to adapt tone, dot size, intensity, distribution and density to the real hair, skin, treated area and each person's goal.

Important: micropigmentation does not treat the cause of alopecia and does not replace medical assessment when a scalp or hair condition is present. It is an aesthetic solution designed to improve visual appearance.

When you still have hair: density effect

The pigment dots are distributed between existing hairs to visually darken the background and reduce the difference in tone between skin and hair. This can make the scalp less visible and the coverage appear more even.

Method

How is scalp micropigmentation performed?

The result is built progressively. I do not try to saturate the scalp in the first session; I prefer to see how the skin responds and increase density in a controlled way.

Personalised assessment

I assess the actual hair loss, existing hair, scalp, treatment area, background and goal. In density cases, a video parting the hair helps me evaluate the thinner areas more accurately.

Design and planning

We define the right approach: density, shaved effect, crown integration, scar camouflage or complementing a transplant. Tone, dot size and intensity are selected accordingly.

First session

A conservative, even base is created. Avoiding excessive density at the start leaves room to adjust the result according to the actual pigment retention.

Adjustment sessions

After the skin has evolved, the necessary areas are reinforced, contrasts are balanced and integration is refined. Treatment is usually planned over several sessions.

Realistic expectations

What scalp micropigmentation can achieve — and what it cannot

A good indication starts with understanding the possibilities and limitations. The aim is to achieve a credible visual improvement, not to promise a result the technique cannot deliver.

It can help to

  • Visually reduce scalp contrast in areas with lower density.
  • Create the appearance of very short hair with a shaved effect.
  • Visually reinforce the crown, top or frontal area in suitable cases.
  • Complement certain hair-transplant results.
  • Optically camouflage certain scars or donor-area contrasts.

It does not

  • It does not generate new hair.
  • It does not increase the actual number of follicles.
  • It does not stop or cure alopecia.
  • It does not replace a dermatological diagnosis when a hair or scalp condition is present.
  • Not every case is suitable: prior assessment is essential.

Frequently asked questions

Questions before scalp micropigmentation

How many sessions are needed?

Treatment is carried out over several sessions to build the result progressively. The exact number depends on the size of the area, skin type, pigment retention and final goal.

Can it be done with long hair?

Yes. In suitable cases, treatment can be performed between existing hairs to reduce the contrast between hair and scalp. It does not increase the number of hairs; it creates the optical effect of greater density.

Is it compatible with a hair transplant?

It can be. In selected cases it can serve as a visual complement when the scalp remains visible or there are differences between areas.

How long does it last?

Duration varies according to skin type, sun exposure, habits and individual response. Over time, the pigment gradually fades and maintenance may be required.
